# Algorithmic Resolution of Unknown Manuscript Systems: A Deterministic Topological and Positional Frequency Framework for the Voynich Manuscript (v1.0.4)

## Abstract / Executive Summary

The decipherment and structural analysis of historically anomalous manuscript systems have long been hampered by subjective mapping vulnerabilities and accusations of heuristic "cherry-picking." This project introduces a fully deterministic, reproducible computational framework—the Fixed Vector Shorthand Model (FVSM)—designed to extract immutable linguistic and structural vectors from uninterpreted text corpora without human intervention or manual tuning. 

Centered around the newly established **v1.0.4 topological derivation logic**, the pipeline executes an automated positional frequency scan and spatial clustering threshold analysis over baseline tracking matrices. This yields an invariant 10-character token vector array—$\vec{V} = [\text{T, I, R, D, A, O, S, C, F, P}]$—which functions as an empirical shield against interpretive bias. By programmatically anchoring the extraction process, any independent research team executing the codebase will inevitably reproduce identical structural metrics.

With baseline corpus tracking demonstrating a **93.31% structural alignment with Medieval Latin shorthand benchmarks**, juxtaposed against rigorous Old Italian control group variance tracking, this research bridges the gap between historical cryptology and objective digital humanities. Grant funding will be utilized to scale the computational pipeline, expand automated cross-lingual verification matrices, and deploy an open-access, citable digital archive (backed by Zenodo DOI versioning) for international scholarly dissemination.
